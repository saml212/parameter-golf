Detailed approach (max 2,500 chars):

I found that post-training int8 quantization is the dominant BPB bottleneck on 8xH100 -- the quant penalty alone (0.014 BPB at default settings) is larger than most architectural improvements combined. Everything I've built follows from this.

The main discovery: setting WARMDOWN_ITERS=20000 (well beyond the ~12,200 actual steps) so the entire run is one long LR decay. The model never trains at full learning rate -- it starts at 61% of peak and decays to near-zero. This produces tighter weight distributions that survive int8 rounding with far less damage. I swept 10 warmdown values from 2400 to 30000 and mapped the full curve:

WD=2400: 1.2284 BPB (quant penalty ~0.014)
WD=8000: 1.2237 BPB (penalty ~0.007)
WD=15000: 1.2187 BPB (penalty ~0.005)
WD=20000: 1.2167 BPB (penalty ~0.001) <-- sweet spot
WD=30000: 1.2174 BPB (overshoots, LR decays too fast)

This connects to recent work on training dynamics and quantization (arXiv:2510.06213) but pushes into a "100% warmdown" regime nobody has explored. The mechanism is clean: smaller late-training LR steps = less weight jitter = tighter per-row distributions = finer int8 grid resolution.

On top of that I keep the tied embedding in fp16 during int8 export instead of quantizing it. This matrix does double duty as input embedding and output projection, so int8 damage hits twice. Costs ~500KB, offset by narrowing MLP hidden from 1024 to 992. This brought the total down to 1.2154 BPB.

I also found that eval extrapolation behaves differently depending on training convergence. eval@2048 was +0.048 BPB on undertrained 1xH100 models but neutral-to-negative on well-trained 8xH100 models. The sweet spot is 1.375x (eval@1408). I tested the full curve from 1024 to 2048.

One more interaction: MUON_BACKEND_STEPS=5 beats 7 at WD=20000, even though 7 beats 5 at WD=2400. When warmdown already smooths weights, faster steps matter more than better gradient orthogonalization.

I also tested SwiGLU (no gain at iso-params), int6 mixed-precision (failed catastrophically with high LR, likely needs smoother training), and EMA (doesn't help at any decay). These negative results informed my understanding of what matters at this scale.

Result: 1.2154 BPB. PR #61: https://github.com/openai/parameter-golf/pull/61

Detailed approach (max 2,500 chars):

Follow-up to my earlier application (was at 1.2154). I've since run about 60 experiments over 48 hours and hit 1.1574 BPB — #1 on the leaderboard right now (PR #114). Burned through my personal compute getting here so I'm applying again.

The short version of how I got here, in three stages:

First I figured out that post-quant int8 damage was the biggest problem — bigger than any architectural change. I found that cranking WARMDOWN_ITERS way past the actual step count (20000 vs ~12200 steps) forces the LR to decay the entire run, which makes weights tighter and way easier to quantize. Swept 10 warmdown values to map this out. Also started keeping the tied embedding in fp16 since it pulls double duty as the output head. (PR #61, 1.2154)

Then I got sliding window eval working and started training at seq_len=2048 with higher momentum. Interesting thing I found: train@2048 and train@4096 give basically identical BPB once you have sliding window — the window handles long context at eval, so the model just needs to learn local patterns. 2048 gets more steps so it's strictly better. Also did a granular clip sweep and landed on 0.3 for long sequences, and batch=786K turned out optimal (swept 393K to 1M). (PR #96, 1.1764)

Finally I got int6 quantization working. My first attempt months ago was a disaster — I was rounding int8 values to multiples of 4, which is the wrong approach. Proper int6 means quantizing directly to a ±31 range with per-row scales. This freed about 25% artifact space so I could triple the MLP hidden dim to 1536 (21.8M params in 16MB). Also keep the last two layers' key projections in fp16 since late attention needs precise token discrimination. (PR #114, 1.1574)

I also tried and killed a bunch of stuff: NorMuon (110ms/step, throughput death), multi-token prediction (86ms/step, same problem), ALBERT embedding factorization (torch.compile choked on it), SwiGLU (no gain at matched params), EMA, depth recurrence, KV head reduction. The lesson I keep relearning: on a 10-min budget, anything that slows your step by more than ~10% is net negative. Throughput is king.
